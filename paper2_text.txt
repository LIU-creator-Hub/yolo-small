ABSTRACT
Steel surface defect detection is a critical component of quality control in intelligent manufacturing,
yet it faces two fundamental challenges. First, defect morphologies are extremely heterogeneous:
high-frequencyweakcracksandlow-frequencylarge-areapatchesexhibitentangledspatial–frequency
characteristics, causing subtle defects to be overwhelmed by background texture. Second, industrial
edgedevicesimposestringentcomputationalconstraints.Featureconfusionandweakenedgeometric
perceptionfrequentlyarisewhenlightweightmodelsreducefeaturedimensionality,makingitdifficult
to balance the detection accuracy of non-rigid defects against deployment efficiency.
Toaddresstheselimitations,thispaperproposesSPL-YOLO,alightweighthigh-accuracynetwork
based on multiscale spatial–frequency collaboration. It follows a multilevel strategy of frequency-
domain decoupling, geometric modulation, and morphology-aware focusing. A spectral decoupling
anomaly-aware convolution (SDAConv) is first introduced at the fine-grained P3 feature level. An
average-poolingbackgroundsmootherextractshigh-frequencyresidualsandexplicitlystrengthensthe
edge topology ofsmall defects. A partial spatialmorphological modulation module (P_SMD) isthen
deployed at the P3, P4, and P5 levels. Its 50% channel-splitting sparse deformation strategy, together
with spatial morphological gating, dynamically fits non-rigid defect contours at low computational
cost. Finally, layer-wise morphological attention (LYMA) is embedded at all three detection levels.
Three parallel paths based on strip, dilated, and large-kernel depthwise convolutions adaptively
enhance responses to representative defect morphologies.
On NEU-DET, SPL-YOLO achieves 79.0% mAP@0.5 with only 2.01 million parameters and
5.2 GFLOPs. Compared with YOLOv11n, it reduces the parameter count by 30.9% and computation
by 32.5%, while improving mAP@0.5 by 2.3 percentage points and maintaining 76.07 FPS. Cross-
scenario experiments on GC10-DET yield 69.3% mAP@0.5, demonstrating strong generalization.
The proposed design provides an efficient route for optimizing industrial defect detectors under
resource constraints.
1. Introduction
Withtherapiddevelopmentofindustrialautomationand
intelligentmanufacturing,machine-vision-basedsurfacede-
fect detection has become essential for quality control and
yield improvement in the steel industry[1,2]. During hot and
cold rolling, coupled factors such as rolling stress, pro-
cessfluctuations,andenvironmentalimpuritiescanproduce
crazing, scratches, inclusions, patches, pitted surfaces, and
rolled-in scale. These defects exhibit strong morphological
heterogeneityandinterclasssimilarity.Someareweakhigh-
frequency cracks only a few pixels wide, whereas others are
low-frequency patches occupying nearly half of an image.
Their visual signatures are often obscured by metallic color
and rolling texture, presenting a major challenge for auto-
mated inspection[3,4].
Single-stage detectors represented by the YOLO family
have been widely adopted for industrial defect detection

2. YOLOv11 Object Detection Model
YOLOv11 is a representative real-time single-stage de-
tector[11]. Its topology consists of a backbone, neck, and
decoupled detection head, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Figure 1:Architecture of the YOLOv11 object detection
model.
The backbone is built on an improved cross-stage par-
tial architecture and integrates C3k2 and C2PSA mod-
ules.Cross-stageconnectionsreduceredundantcomputation
while strengthening deep channel and spatial representa-
tions. At the end of the backbone, spatial pyramid pooling–
fast(SPPF)aggregatesmultiscaleglobalcontextandoutputs
P3,P4,andP5featuremapsatdownsamplingratiosof8,16,
and 32, respectively.
The neck combines a feature pyramid with a path ag-
gregationnetwork [12].Atop-downbilinearupsamplingpath
propagates strong high-level semantics, while a bottom-up
convolutional path transfers high-resolution detail. Cross-
level enhancement and skip connections alleviate spatial-
detail loss in deep features. Finally, an anchor-free decou-
pled head separates classification and bounding-box regres-
sion[13]. It predicts center offsets and box scales at P3–
P5 and uses distribution focal loss for robust multiscale
regression.
3. Proposed SPL-YOLO Model
SPL-YOLO retains the backbone–neck–head organiza-
tion of YOLOv11n while adopting a coordinated strat-
egy of frequency decoupling, geometric modulation, and
morphology-aware focusing. As shown in Fig. 2, SDAConv
strengthens weak high-frequency responses at P3; P_SMD
balances geometric perception and computation at P3–P5;
and LYMA supplies morphology-specific attention at all
threedetectionlevels.Thesecomponentsactatcomplemen-
tarystages,reducingbothparametersandcomputationwhile
improving accuracy.

3.3. Layer-Wise Morphological Attention

fields and generic channel/spatial attention can incorporate
excessive background for thin scratches and cracks, yet
remain too limited to cover large irregular patches[16–18].
LYMAinsteadembedsindustrialmorphologypriorsthrough
three parallel paths and is deployed at P3, P4, and P5.

4.4. Comparison of attention mechanisms
LYMA was compared with coordinate attention (CA),
contextanchorattention(CAA),globalattentionmechanism
(GAM), efficient channel attention (ECA), and convolu-
tional block attention module (CBAM). Each module was
insertedatthesamenetworklocationsandtrainedwithiden-
tical hyperparameters. Table 2 shows that LYMA achieves
the best mAP@0.5 and recall.
CAA reduces mAP@0.5 to 76.2%, indicating that in-
appropriate contextual aggregation can damage weak high-
frequency structures. GAM improves recall but raises com-
putation by approximately 16.9%. CBAM reaches 78.4%
mAP@0.5withlittleextracomputation,butitsrigidpooling
limits representation of strongly anisotropic defects. LYMA
obtains 79.0% mAP@0.5 and improves recall by 7.5 per-
centage points over the baseline, while retaining real-time
performance.
Grad-CAM visualizations in Fig. 7 provide qualitative
evidence. Conventional mechanisms exhibit false boundary
activation, fragmented hotspots, or diffuse background re-
sponses on irregular patches. In contrast, LYMA concen-

5. Conclusion
This paper proposed SPL-YOLO to address extreme
spatial–frequency heterogeneity in steel defects and strin-
gent resource constraints in industrial edge inspection. Its
multilevel architecture combines SDAConv for frequency-
domain enhancement, P_SMD for efficient non-rigid ge-
ometric modeling, and LYMA for morphology-prior-
guided focusing. Together, these modules alleviate spatial–
frequency coupling, reconcile deformation perception with
efficiency, and reduce heterogeneous feature confusion.
On NEU-DET, SPL-YOLO achieves 79.0% mAP@0.5
and 79.3% precision with 2.01 M parameters and 5.2
GFLOPs. Relative to YOLOv11n, the parameter count and
computationdecreaseby30.9%and32.5%,whilemAP@0.5
increases by 2.3 percentage points and inference remains at
76.07 FPS.
OnGC10-DET,themodelobtainsthehighestmAP@0.5
of 69.3%. Heatmaps and qualitative results further show
improved localization of irregular contours and stronger
responses to weak defects.
Performance under extreme reflective glare and se-
vere surface tearing remains an open issue. Future work
will combine pruning and knowledge distillation to re-
duce storage and energy consumption, and will extend the
spatial–frequency collaborative strategy to aluminum, cop-
per, printed circuit boards, and other industrial materials.
